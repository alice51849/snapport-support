Why the size is the tricky part

US photo counters and kiosks are set up for 2×2 inch photos and standard prints such as 4×6. A 35×45 mm photo is narrower and taller than 2×2 (51×51 mm), and 33×48 mm is narrower still, so trimming a 2×2 print by hand rarely lands on the right proportions. Building the sheet at the exact metric size first, then printing it at 100%, keeps every copy correct.

Step by step

  1. Take the photo against a plain, light wall in even daylight, looking straight at the camera.
  2. In Snapport, pick the template (for example 35×45 mm) and line up the head-top, eye-line and chin guides.
  3. Change the background to white or gray if the requirement asks for it, and compare before and after.
  4. Create a 4×6 print sheet; Snapport places multiple copies with cut guides.
  5. Export the sheet as JPEG or PDF and order a regular 4×6 print. Turn off cropping, borders and any “fit” option.
  6. Cut along the guides and measure one photo with a ruler before you submit it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a drugstore print a 35x45 mm photo?

Usually not as a size option, but they can print a 4×6 sheet. If the sheet already contains 35×45 mm photos at the right scale, you just order a normal 4×6 print and cut them out.

My photos came out slightly too small or too large. Why?

The print service scaled or cropped the sheet. Choose the plain 4×6 size, disable auto-crop, borders and “fit to print,” and upload the exported file without editing it in another app.

Can I mix sizes on one sheet?

The simplest way is one sheet per size: build a 4×6 sheet of 35×45 mm copies, then a second sheet for the other size, and order both as regular 4×6 prints.

Is a home-made photo accepted for a Schengen or Chinese visa?

Requirements vary by country, consulate and application type, and some applications take the photo or a digital upload at the appointment. Snapport provides common templates and guides; it does not guarantee acceptance and is not affiliated with any government. Check the official instructions first.

Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything is processed on your iPhone, with no account, ads, tracking or cloud. The only network use is the optional, user-initiated template update check.
