How the copy count works

The tool tries the paper upright and sideways, keeps every photo upright, and counts the largest grid that fits after the border and gaps. Gaps make cutting easier but can cost copies: two 2-inch photos already fill the 4-inch side of a 4×6 print, so any gap or border on that side drops the count from six to two. Borderless prints are often enlarged a little, which trims the outer edge, so a layout that fills the sheet edge to edge leaves no room for error. When you order the print, turn off cropping, borders and “fit to page”, then measure one photo with a ruler before cutting.

Frequently asked questions

How many 2x2 photos fit on a 4x6 print?

Six, in a 2 by 3 grid, when the photos touch each other and the print has no border. Any gap between photos or white border drops it to two, because 2 + 2 inches already fills the 4-inch side.

How many 35x45 mm photos fit on a 4x6 print?

Eight, in a 4 by 2 grid on a landscape 4x6 sheet, with no gaps and no border. With 2 mm gaps and no border, eight still fit.

Is a 4x6 print the same as 10x15 cm?

Almost. A 4x6 inch print is 101.6 x 152.4 mm, slightly larger than 100 x 150 mm. Pick the paper you will actually print on: 2x2 inch photos fit six to a borderless 4x6 print, but only two on 10x15 cm paper, which is 1.6 mm narrower.

Does this tool upload anything?

No. The sizes are built into the page and the math runs in your browser. There is no photo upload and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
